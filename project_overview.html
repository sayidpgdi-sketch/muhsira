01 · Rationale & problem

Reach is not the same as customer flow.

Small businesses increasingly use paid social media advertising, including Meta and Google Ads, to reach targeted audiences. Yet digital impressions and clicks do not always result in store visits, enquiries, or purchases.

The synopsis highlights a local research question: why can paid campaigns struggle to build the trust and personal connection that customers may feel from influencer-led recommendations, particularly in Malappuram's food and hospitality market?

Central research question

How effectively do paid social media advertisements convert digital visibility into real customer flow, and what factors influence that conversion?

02 · Research objectives

What the study aims to establish

01

Understand advertising trends

Identify the paid social platforms, ad formats, and content approaches used by small-scale entrepreneurs in Malappuram.

02

Evaluate campaign effectiveness

Compare perceived paid-ad performance with actual customer flow, and consider organic and influencer-led marketing.

03

Identify technical challenges

Explore budget management, audience targeting, content quality, and campaign optimisation difficulties.

04

Assess consumer trust

Understand consumer perceptions of sponsored ads compared with personal recommendations in the local market.

A descriptive and analytical study.

05 · Literature & research gap

From online engagement to measurable outcomes.

Social media and SME digitalisation

The synopsis refers to Kaplan & Haenlein (2010) and Kietzmann et al. (2011) to frame social platforms as tools for participation, sharing, identity, and brand-building.

Trust, sponsored content, and consumer behaviour

The proposal considers social proof, ad fatigue, and the possibility that personal recommendations may be perceived differently from sponsored posts.

The local research gap

The study focuses on the gap between online reach or impressions and real-world footfall in small-scale businesses in Malappuram, especially food and hospitality.

07 · Scope & limitations

A defined local study.

The proposal focuses on Malappuram District, with particular attention to the food and hospitality sector, including cafes, restaurants, and home bakers.

  • Geographical scope: findings may not generalise to other districts or states.
  • Sample size: planned sample of 50 entrepreneurs and 100 consumers.
  • Time constraint: limited academic timeframe may restrict long-term analysis.
  • Respondent bias: self-reported responses may be subjective or incomplete.
  • Platform changes: changing advertising algorithms may affect campaign performance over time.

Research deliverable

Evidence-led recommendations for local businesses.

The proposed report is organised into five chapters: Introduction; Review of Literature; Research Methodology; Data Analysis and Interpretation; and Findings, Suggestions, and Conclusion.
