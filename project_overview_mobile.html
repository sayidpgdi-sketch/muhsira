M.Com · MCOP–001 · Research synopsis

A study on the impact of paid social media advertising on small-scale businesses.

Examining whether paid social campaigns turn online visibility into customer trust, physical footfall, and actual sales across Malappuram District.

Malappuram DistrictFood & HospitalityDescriptive & Analytical
Entrepreneurs50Small-scale businesses using paid ads
Consumers100Social media users
Owner interviews5In-depth case interviews
Consumer insights10Qualitative interactions

Central research question

How effectively do paid social media ads convert digital reach into real customer flow, and which factors influence that conversion?

Research focus

Beyond impressions and clicks.

The study explores ad spending, content quality, audience targeting, consumer trust, and the difference between sponsored posts and personal recommendations.

01 · Rationale & problem

Reach is not the same as customer flow.

Small-scale businesses increasingly invest in paid social media advertising, but online impressions and clicks may not result in enquiries, store visits, or purchases.

The local research problem

The synopsis highlights a possible gap between digital impressions and actual customer flow in Malappuram, especially in food and hospitality businesses such as cafes, restaurants, and home bakers.

Why it matters

Influencer-led recommendations and personal trust can sometimes generate stronger customer response than sponsored posts. The study explores possible reasons, including content, targeting, ad fatigue, and credibility.

Research question

Do paid campaigns bring customers through the door?

The research aims to connect online visibility with real-world business outcomes, not assume that likes or impressions alone prove success.

02 · Research objectives

What the study aims to establish.

01
Analyse advertising trends

Identify the paid platforms, preferred formats, and content approaches used by small-scale entrepreneurs in Malappuram.

02
Evaluate campaign effectiveness

Examine whether paid advertisements generate actual customer flow compared with organic and influencer-led marketing.

03
Identify technical challenges

Explore ad-budget management, targeting, content quality, and campaign optimisation issues.

04
Assess consumer trust

Understand consumer perceptions of sponsored advertising versus personal recommendations in the local market.

03 · Research methodology

A descriptive and analytical design.

The proposal uses convenience sampling and a triangulated plan combining quantitative surveys with qualitative interviews.

50 entrepreneurs

Structured survey of small-scale business owners using paid social media ads.

100 consumers

Survey of social media users who engage with local business content.

5 business interviews

In-depth case interviews to understand operational and advertising challenges.

10 consumer interactions

Qualitative conversations to explore buying triggers and recommendation trust.

Data sources

Primary data: structured Google Forms questionnaires and interview schedules. Secondary data: academic journals, industry reports, textbooks, and relevant online resources.

Study universe: small-scale entrepreneurs and active social media users in Malappuram District, with a specific focus on food and hospitality.

04 · Study variables

Campaign inputs and business outcomes.

Independent variables

Campaign inputs

Paid ad spendPhoto/video qualityAudience targeting

These are the advertising factors examined in relation to campaign performance.

Dependent variables

Business outcomes

Customer flowPhysical footfallBrand trustConversion rate

The study considers whether campaign inputs are associated with outcomes that matter to local businesses.

05 · Literature review & research gap

From engagement to evidence.

SME digitalisation

The synopsis cites Kaplan & Haenlein (2010) and Kietzmann et al. (2011) when discussing social media, participation, sharing, and brand-building.

Trust and consumer behaviour

The proposal considers social proof, ad fatigue, sponsored content, and the influence of peer recommendations on consumer choices.

Research gap

The study focuses on the gap between online reach or impressions and actual physical customer footfall among small-scale businesses in Malappuram.

06 · Data analysis plan

Planned tools for interpreting responses.

Percentage analysis

Summarise quantitative survey responses and compare patterns across respondent groups.

Charts & comparative tables

Present collected data through pie charts, bar graphs, and comparative tables.

Case summaries

Organise qualitative interview responses into summaries of campaign challenges, consumer trust, and buying triggers.

Data integrity

Findings come after data collection.

No results are invented or presented as completed. Real charts and conclusions should be added only after actual responses have been collected and analysed.

07 · Scope & limitations

A focused study within a defined area.

Geographical scope

Malappuram District, with special attention to cafes, restaurants, and home bakers in the food and hospitality sector.

Limitations identified in the synopsis

  • Findings may not generalise beyond Malappuram District.
  • Planned sample is limited to 50 entrepreneurs and 100 consumers.
  • Academic time constraints limit long-term analysis.
  • Self-reported data may include respondent bias.
  • Social media algorithms change frequently.

Planned report chapters

1. Introduction · 2. Review of Literature · 3. Research Methodology · 4. Data Analysis and Interpretation · 5. Findings, Suggestions, and Conclusion.
